04 · The exit argument

People love Sara.
That is the asset and the risk.

Patients are loyal to Sara because of who she is and how she works. That is rare and valuable. It is also the single biggest risk to the long-term value of the practice. Imagine the day she wants to sell, slow down, take six months off, or bring in a second practitioner. Two versions of that day, depending on what was built.

Without a brand

Sara is the business
  • The new owner inherits a name, a space, a client list, equipment.
  • Every patient was choosing Sara, not the practice.
  • A replacement practitioner has no shared voice, palette, story, or visual identity to step into.
  • Retention drops the moment Sara is no longer the face.
  • Buyers know this, so they discount the price aggressively.

"I am buying Sara's clients, and Sara is leaving."Valued as: equipment + lease.

With a brand

Sara leads the business
  • The new owner inherits a recognised name, a defined voice, a visual system, a content history.
  • Patients chose the practice for what it stands for - Sara was the first proof, not the whole proof.
  • A new practitioner steps into an existing identity rather than starting from zero.
  • Retention continues because the experience is consistent.
  • Buyers pay for the brand equity, not just the equipment.

"I am buying a practice that runs, with or without the founder."Valued as: equipment + lease + brand + goodwill.

07 · The premium-price lever

Same day. Same hands.
Same treatments. Better paid.

The opportunity in this rebrand is not more patients. It is more margin, on the patients you already see and on the new ones the brand will bring in. Strong brands in private healthcare consistently command 15 to 25 percent higher prices than equally qualified, weaker-presented competitors, because the visual and verbal world around the work finally matches the work itself.

Consistent branding compounds into pricing power across every surface the patient touches - the booking email, the waiting room, the printed materials, the follow-up. None of it requires more of Sara's time.

  • No additional hours in the calendar.
  • No additional acquisition cost.
  • Higher show-up rate, because a serious brand creates a serious appointment.
  • Lower price sensitivity in reviews and word-of-mouth, because patients describe the experience, not the invoice.

04 · Current brand audit

Current analysis.
And the potential inside it.

Your brand as it stands today -
and what it's already capable of.

A section-by-section read of quirosasun.com. What it communicates well, where trust slips away, and where the biggest opportunities are waiting.

01 · Hero

What it communicates
  • Local and findable. "Quiropractico Bilbao" positions clearly for search - visitors know immediately where the practice is.
  • Real people, real work. Photography shows Sara in an actual session - not stock, not staged. That's rare and valuable.
  • Approachable. The overall tone is friendly, not cold or clinical.
Weak points
  • The green overlay buries the photography. The strongest asset - Sara in a real session - is filtered green. It reads as a health app, not a specialist practice.
  • The logo signals the wrong category. A green puzzle-piece reads as health insurance, not a serious chiropractic specialist.
  • "Quiropractico Bilbao" is an SEO headline, not a brand statement. It could belong to any of the twenty other practices in the city. No emotional hook, no differentiation.
Potential
  • Remove the tint and let the photography breathe. The session images are genuinely compelling - they don't need filtering.
  • A single line that no other chiropractor in Bilbao can say would change everything. The headline is the biggest single lever on the site.

02 + 03 · About Sara & Conditions

What it communicates
  • Sara is the practice. "Soy Sara" is exactly the right instinct - personal, warm, direct. The visitor knows who they're dealing with immediately.
  • Credentials are visible. Barcelona College, master's from Pompeu Fabra, doctorate from the European Academy.
  • Broad clinical scope. The scrolling conditions marquee signals the practice handles many situations, not just back pain.
Weak points
  • "De confianza" is the most overused phrase in Spanish service businesses. Every dentist, mechanic, and lawyer uses it. It says nothing specific about Sara.
  • The credentials are listed, but the story isn't told. Why chiropractic? Why Bilbao? Her most powerful brand asset is buried on another page.
  • The conditions marquee is pure SEO logic, not patient logic. No one thinks "I have dolor de cuello, let me find a site that lists that word." It reads as a keyword dump.
Potential
  • Her father's story - discovering chiropractic at 15 - is rare brand material. It should anchor this section, not sit hidden in "Sobre Mí."
  • Replace conditions with outcome language: "You came in barely able to turn your head. You left standing straighter." Same information, written for the patient.

04 · Educational content

What it communicates
  • Sara explains, not just adjusts. The spine model and clinical setting signal an educational, thoughtful approach to care.
  • Real environment, real tools. The props, the patient, the setting - all authentic. Nothing staged.
  • She invests in patient education. A practitioner who takes time to explain earns a different kind of trust.
Weak points
  • The section drops in without context. No headline, no framing - the visitor doesn't know what they're about to see or why it matters to them.
  • No copy bridges the content to the patient's situation. A powerful image is left to carry everything alone.
Potential
  • A single headline unlocks this section: "Understanding your spine changes how you live in your body."
  • Most practitioners don't explain. Sara does. That deserves to be framed - it's a real differentiator.

05 · Testimonials

What it communicates
  • Patients have transformative, emotional experiences. The reviews are long, specific, and personal - not generic stars.
  • The practice generates real loyalty. "Con ganas de seguir mi tratamiento" - patients don't want to stop. That's a rare signal.
  • Outcomes are life-scale, not symptom-scale. "Cambiar mi relación con mi cuerpo" is a brand claim patients are making on Sara's behalf - unprompted.
Weak points
  • "Esto es lo que Opinan mis Clientes…" is placeholder copy. It doesn't invite you in. It certainly doesn't honor the quality of the reviews beneath it.
  • The slider format hides the strongest asset on the site. Only two reviews visible at a time. The content is exceptional - treated as an afterthought.
  • Visual hierarchy is flat. Every word carries the same weight. Nothing pulls out, nothing lands harder than anything else.
Potential
  • Lead with a full-width pull-quote from the best review, typeset large - before any heading. Let the patient speak first.
  • These reviews are the most powerful brand asset on the site. They're being underused by a significant margin.

06 · FAQ

What it communicates
  • Sara anticipates objections and is transparent. The FAQ shows real awareness of what first-time patients fear - and answers honestly.
  • Educational and non-intimidating. A practice that explains this clearly is one that respects its patients.
  • The questions are the right questions. "Are adjustments painful?" and "Can I go if pregnant?" are exactly what an anxious first-time visitor needs answered.
Weak points
  • Visually, this is the most off-brand section on the site. Green rounded accordion panels look like a generic healthcare template - no connection to the warmth above.
  • Green saturation peaks exactly here. The accordion headers are full vivid green - it overwhelms the content and clashes with the tone the practice wants.
  • "Preguntas Frecuentes" is a label, not an invitation. It does nothing for the curious or undecided visitor.
Potential
  • The content is strong - it only needs a visual system. A typography-driven design without accordion widgets would feel significantly more premium.
  • Rename to something warmer: "Before your first visit" - same content, completely different emotional entry point.

07 · Location & closing

What it communicates
  • Local and findable. The map places the practice clearly in Bilbao.
  • Functional and direct. No ambiguity about where to go.
Weak points
  • The site ends with a Google Maps widget. After building warmth through Sara's story, testimonials, and FAQ - the page closes with a utility embed. It's an anticlimax.
  • No closing voice. Sara doesn't say goodbye. No final line stays with the visitor after they close the tab.
  • No final CTA. The page's last action is "click the map" - not "book an appointment." The moment is left on the table.
Potential
  • Close with Sara's voice before the map: "The first step is often the hardest. I'll take care of the rest."
  • A prominent "Book a first visit" button here, after all the trust has been built, would convert everything the page worked toward.
08 · Instagram audit

374 posts. One missing thread.

The content is there. The system isn't yet.

3,500 followers, consistent posting, real patient stories, Reels that generate emotional comments from other cities. The raw material is exceptional - what's missing is the visual and strategic layer that would make it unmistakable.

Profile · First impression

What works
  • 374 posts, 3,500 followers. For a solo specialist practice in Bilbao, this is a genuinely active and committed presence. Consistency alone is rare.
  • The bio has personality. "Todo lo que pasa por tu columna" is a better hook than most chiropractors manage. It leads with the patient's body, not a service list.
  • Highlights show humanity. Uganda, India, Marruecos - Sara's travels signal a person, not just a practitioner. That's a meaningful brand signal.
  • Reviews highlight is present. Pulling Google reviews into a dedicated highlight shows awareness of social proof.
Weak points
  • The profile photo distances instead of connecting. Sara holding the branded green card is corporate - the most successful health practitioners use a direct, warm face. The first pixel of trust is lost.
  • "Ajustamos tu columna vertebral" is too clinical. It describes a procedure, not an outcome. A patient doesn't want their column adjusted - they want to stop waking up in pain.
  • The Highlight covers look default. No branded palette, no consistent icon language - they feel like placeholders compared to the content inside them.
  • 593 following for 3,500 followers. A high ratio signals no curation strategy - the algorithm reads this as a low-authority account.
Potential
  • A warm, direct headshot as the profile photo would immediately raise the perceived warmth and trust of the account - before anyone reads a word.
  • Bio rewrite: keep "Todo lo que pasa por tu columna" as the hook, replace "Ajustamos tu columna vertebral" with an outcome line. Add a booking CTA link.
  • Branded Highlight covers in the new palette (bone, moss, clay) would immediately signal a premium, intentional presence.

Grid · Visual system

What works
  • Real patient diversity. Babies, elderly patients, athletes, adults - the grid communicates without words that everyone is welcome and every body is different.
  • Authentic clinic environment. The white walls, natural light, and actual treatment sessions convey honesty. Nothing feels staged or stock.
  • The green treatment table is an accidental anchor. It appears in most photos and creates a subconscious thread of visual continuity - even without intention.
Weak points
  • The grid reads as a medical record archive. Post after post of treatment photos with no visual rhythm, no breathing room, no contrast. The eye has nowhere to rest.
  • Zero visual system. No consistent edit, no color grading, no composition logic. Photos vary in exposure, warmth, and crop with no unifying treatment.
  • No content type differentiation. Educational posts, treatment photos, and personal content all look identical in the grid. There's no visual signal that tells a visitor "this post teaches something" vs. "this is a case story."
  • No intentional grid composition. Top accounts in this space alternate text cards, photography, and Reel covers to create visual rhythm. There's none here.
Potential
  • A simple Lightroom preset applied consistently to all photos would unify the grid overnight - warm, slightly desaturated, soft highlights. No radical change needed.
  • Introduce 2–3 visual content pillars with distinct looks: treatment photos (photography), education (text card in brand palette), patient stories (portrait with name). The grid becomes navigable.
  • One text card every 3–4 posts would break the density and give the grid visual air - while also performing better in reach.

Content types · Reels & series

What works
  • Reels are present and generating reach. Posts like "no sigas viendo el video" and "este pinchazo" use hook-first logic correctly - this is exactly what the algorithm rewards.
  • The patient case series is a standout format. "El caso de Aster", "El caso de Marta", "El caso de Jon", "El caso de Unaí", "El caso de Fran" - named, personal, story-driven. This is the format that builds real trust and sets Sara apart from every anonymous clinic account.
  • Educational content is emerging. "Neuroplasticidad", "Mitos y verdades", "¿Dolor Crónico?" show willingness to teach. This is the content that gets saved and shared - the highest-value metric on Instagram.
  • "Primera visita" content addresses the main conversion barrier - what does it feel like to come in for the first time? Answering that question removes the biggest obstacle to booking.
Weak points
  • Reel covers are inconsistent. Some have text overlays in different fonts, sizes, and positions. Some are raw video frames. There's no cover template - which means the grid impact of Reels is lost.
  • The case series has no visual identity. "El caso de Aster" and "El caso de Marta" look completely different from each other. The format is strong - the execution doesn't signal "this is a series."
  • Educational content is underproduced. Topics like neuroplasticity and chronic pain deserve more than a text overlay on a random frame. The content quality is there; the presentation isn't.
Potential
  • The patient case series is the single biggest content opportunity. A consistent template - portrait photo, "El caso de [Name]" in the new brand typography, same colour system - would make the series immediately recognisable and bingeable.
  • Design one Reel cover template in the new brand palette. Apply retroactively to all Reels. The grid transforms overnight.
  • The educational topics are exactly what high-performing health accounts cover. "Neuroplasticidad" and "¿Dolor Crónico?" as carousels with branded slides would get saved - and saves are the metric that compounds reach over time.

Reels · Thumbnails, copy & comments

What works
  • The caption copy is genuinely strong. "Muchas personas llegan buscando un cambio inmediato…" frames chiropractic as a process not a quick fix, and closes with a direct question - textbook hook structure.
  • Hook text overlays on the video frame work. "no sigas viendo el video", "este pinchazo", "molestia vale puede ser" - short, provocative, conversational. They stop the scroll before anyone reads the caption.
  • Comments are emotional and unprompted. "El milagro eres tú con esas manos", "Benditas manos 🙏" - patients are volunteering brand language Sara isn't even using herself. That's a rare signal.
  • The content reaches beyond Bilbao. "¿Podrías recomendarme alguien en mi ciudad?" - the Reels are generating demand Sara can't geographically serve. That's a real authority signal.
Weak points
  • No brand identity visible anywhere in the videos. Not a logo, not a colour, not a typeface - nothing in any of the four Reels signals Quirosasun. Someone who shares the video takes Sara's content with zero attribution.
  • The thumbnails are raw video frames, not designed covers. In the grid view every Reel looks like a frozen accident - a mid-movement shot, an awkward crop, a patient mid-adjustment. There's no intention in what the visitor sees before they tap.
  • Hook text overlays have no visual consistency. Different fonts, different positions, different sizes across the four videos. They work individually but create noise in the grid.
  • No logo watermark or closing frame. The most shared Reels in this category end with a clean branded frame: name, handle, call to action. None of these do.
  • The comments are more powerful than the posts. "El milagro eres tú" is a brand line - but it lives in a comment, not in Sara's own copy.
Potential
  • Add a subtle logo watermark (top corner, low opacity) to every Reel. One small change - every share becomes a brand impression.
  • Design one cover template per content type: a branded still frame with the hook text in the new typography. The grid reads as a system, not a collection of accidents.
  • Add a 2-second closing frame to every Reel: name, handle, "Quirosasun · Bilbao" in the brand palette. This is the most-replicated pattern among the top health accounts in Spanish.
  • The patient comment language - "esas manos", "el milagro", "cambiar mi relación con mi cuerpo" - is ready-made brand copy. Move it from the comments into Sara's own voice.
  • Pin the 3 strongest Reels to the top of the grid. Best hook, best case story, best educational - the full funnel visible before anyone scrolls.
