# PART 1 — HONEST AUDIT

## What the brand has going for it

A founder with a real story. Sara has a clinical pedigree most chiropractors do not (Barcelona College, Pompeu Fabra master, EAC doctorate, Syntropy training, pregnancy and pediatric formation) and a humanitarian history (Haiti, Dominican Republic, Sri Lanka, India, Morocco). That combination, clinical seriousness plus lived-in compassion, is rare and ownable. It is currently invisible in the brand.

The testimonials are exceptional. Real specifics, long arcs, emotional language. People do not just say Sara fixed their back. They say she changed their relationship to their body. That is a brand built on outcomes, not promises.

The new physical space (beige, white, dark green, wood, glass, black steel) is a generational leap from the current visual identity. The interior is already operating at a higher level than the brand around it.

## What is holding it back

**The current logo undersells the practice.** The rounded silhouette with puzzle pieces reads as friendly and abstract. It does not communicate spine, nervous system, or care for the body's signal pathway. Sara has told you she wants the logo to transmit the brain-spine connection. The current mark does not do that, and refining its outline will not make it.

**The green is too loud for the audience the new space is courting.** Vivid emerald reads as energetic-but-generic medical clinic, like an app icon. The materials in the new space (wood, glass, black steel, dark olive) signal a quieter, more architectural confidence. The brand colour should match that tone of voice, not compete with it.

**The verbal identity is functional but not distinctive.** "Si tienes columna, esto es para ti" is a good hook, but the rest of the copy is generic chiropractic language. Subluxation, ajustes, sistema nervioso, bienestar. Sara could be any practitioner. There is no specific point of view on the page.

**The brand has no architecture.** No system for how things are named, how the voice operates, what photography looks like, how Instagram differs from the website. Every piece reads like it was made one at a time.

## The opportunity

There is a clear gap in the chiropractic market between two extremes: the medical-clinical category (sterile, white, cold) and the wellness-spa category (candles, "harmony," soft focus). Sara sits in a third position that almost no chiropractor occupies: a precision practice with architectural confidence and clinical depth, treating the patient as a thinking adult. The new interior already proves she is committed to this. The brand needs to catch up.

The three concept directions in Part 3 each express this third position differently. Sara picks one.

# PART 2 — STRATEGIC FOUNDATION

## Who Sara actually serves

Three working audience archetypes, derived from the testimonials and the price point. Sara is not building a high-volume back-pain clinic. She is building a focused practice for people who choose her deliberately.

**1. The recovering high-functioning professional (primary, 40% of practice)**

Late twenties to mid-forties. Desk-based or hybrid work. Has tried physio, massage, maybe osteopathy. Has chronic neck, shoulder, mid-back pain that has become part of identity. Reads, asks questions, expects to understand the plan. Pays for quality. Decides based on credentials and the practitioner feeling competent on the first call. Nekane in the testimonials is this person.

**2. The active body wanting to stay active (secondary, 30%)**

Thirties to sixties. Hikes, runs, lifts, climbs, cycles. Comes in because performance is slipping or a movement that used to be free now hurts. Pragmatic. Wants results and protocol clarity. Not interested in candles. Aritz and Koldo are this person.

**3. The optimiser without a problem (tertiary, 30%, growing)**

Late twenties to fifties, often high earners or wellness-curious. No specific pathology. Comes in because they have read about nervous system regulation, posture, vagal tone, sleep, energy. Treats chiropractic as a long-term investment, not a fix. Likely refers other optimisers. Elena is this person. This segment grows fastest in the new positioning.

What these three share: they are not in crisis, they are choosing. They will not respond to fear-based marketing or claims of miracle results. They want a competent adult to look after them.

## Positioning statement

> For people who take the long view on their health, Quirosasun is the Bilbao chiropractic practice that treats the nervous system as the foundation of how you live. Sara Crespo combines deep clinical training with the steady attention most clinics no longer have time for, in a space designed for thinking, breathing, and being looked after well.

## The brand idea, in one line

**Care for the line that runs you.**

The line is the spine. The spine carries the signal. The signal runs everything. The practice exists to keep the line working. This is the conceptual core that should sit underneath every visual, verbal, and experiential decision.

## Mission, Vision, Values

**Mission.** To restore and protect the connection between the brain and the body, one spine at a time, through clinically grounded chiropractic care delivered with patience and precision.

**Vision.** A Bilbao where serious people understand the spine the way they understand their teeth: a thing you look after for life, not a thing you only visit when it hurts.

**Values.** (Five, in priority order.)

1. *Clinical seriousness.* The work is medical, not magical. Plans are explained. Results are measured.
2. *Patient time.* No rushing. The pace of care is part of the care.
3. *Adult conversation.* Patients are treated as capable of understanding their bodies.
4. *Material honesty.* Real materials, real outcomes, real timeframes. No spa theatre.
5. *Quiet excellence.* The work speaks. The brand does not shout.

## Brand personality

If Quirosasun were a person:

- Calm, not soft.
- Precise, not clinical.
- Warm, not familiar.
- Curious, not chatty.
- Confident, not selling.
- Listens longer than expected. Says less than expected. Both feel deliberate.

## Tone of voice

**Yes:** Specific. Grounded. A little dry. Uses real anatomical language without showing off. Says what the work does and does not do. Comfortable with white space and short sentences.

**No:** Wellness clichés (harmony, holistic, journey, balance). Medical fear-mongering (silent killer, hidden subluxations). Overpromise (transform your life). Exclamation marks. Stock affirmations.

**Examples of voice in action:**

*Old:* "La quiropráctica se enfoca en mantener el bienestar del sistema nervioso mediante ajustes vertebrales que ayudan a liberar interferencias, promoviendo así el mejor funcionamiento del organismo."

*New:* "Chiropractic care looks after one thing: how well your nervous system can talk to the rest of you. The work happens at the spine, because that is where the conversation runs."

*Old:* "¡Pide tu cita ahora!"

*New:* "Book the first conversation."

*Old (Instagram caption template):* "💪 Tu salud es lo más importante! La quiropráctica puede ayudarte a vivir mejor."

*New:* "The spine carries about a hundred million signals an hour. Most of yours are working. Some are not. The work is finding out which."

## Messaging pillars

Four themes that every piece of content should ladder back to. Used to plan Instagram, web copy, workshops, and email.

1. **The signal.** Brain to body, body to brain, and the spine as the conduit. The conceptual heart.
2. **The long view.** Spinal health as a multi-decade practice, not a fix-it visit. Education content lives here.
3. **The room.** The physical experience of being looked after. Photography of the new space, the materials, the small moments. Trust-building.
4. **Sara at work.** Her hands, her training, her missions, her thinking. Credibility and humanity.

# PART 3 — THREE CONCEPT DIRECTIONS

Three different visual and verbal expressions of the strategy above. All three sit in the same strategic territory (precision, calm, adult). They differ in temperature, formality, and how visually present the brand is.

Sara picks one. My recommendation is in Part 4, but each is a complete, defensible direction.

---

## CONCEPT A — *Grounded Sanctuary*

**The feeling.** Warmth without softness. A practice that feels like a thoughtful home. Slow morning light, linen, ceramic, the smell of wood. Calm but not sleepy.

**The room it evokes.** Soft beige walls, oak floors, a single dark olive ceramic vessel, light coming sideways through linen. Reassuring.

**Palette.**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary background | Linen | #EFE9DD |
| Secondary background | Soft Olive Cream | #F2F4EE |
| Mid-ground / accent | Sage Olive | #C1CBB6 |
| Brand colour | Deep Olive Moss | #5F6F4A |
| Anchor / type | Olive Bark | #2F3A23 |
| Highlight | Warm Clay | #C9A57A |

**Why this palette.** It introduces warm beige (Linen and Clay) which the current palette is missing, and softens the olive greens by sitting them against creamy neutrals. Clay gives the system a single warm accent so it does not collapse into one temperature.

**Typography.**
- Headlines: *Canela* or *GT Sectra* (sharp, slightly editorial serif with confidence).
- Body: *Söhne* or *Inter* (clean grotesque, readable at any size).
- Small caps / labels: same body face, tracked wide.

**Logo direction.** Refine the existing silhouette. Thin the outline considerably. Redraw the two puzzle pieces as soft vertebra forms. Wordmark in serif, lowercase, with the descriptor "Chiropractic. Bilbao." in tracked small caps below.

**Photography.** Natural light only. Hands, wood grain, a corner of the room, the side of Sara's face mid-conversation. Never staged smiling-to-camera shots. Nothing shiny.

**Sample copy.** "A practice for the long version of your body."

**References.** Aesop, Susanne Kaufmann, Le Labo's quieter store interiors.

**Trade-off.** This is the safest direction. It works immediately, photographs beautifully, and will not date. But it sits adjacent to a hundred other premium wellness brands. If Sara wants distinctive, this is not it.

## CONCEPT B — *Quiet Clinical*

**The feeling.** A precise, almost architectural practice. Closer to a Japanese surgery or a Scandinavian dental clinic than a wellness centre. Confident, intelligent, sparing.

**The room it evokes.** White walls, polished concrete, a single black steel pendant, a deep olive treatment table, one anatomical drawing framed simply on the wall. The space says: this is where serious work happens.

**Palette.**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary background | Paper White | #F6F4EF |
| Secondary | Soft Olive Cream | #F2F4EE |
| Anchor (type, lines) | Near Black | #1A1C18 |
| Brand colour | Deep Olive Moss | #5F6F4A |
| Quiet accent | Steel Grey | #8B8D85 |
| Reserved highlight | Warm Sand | #D9CDB8 |

**Why this palette.** Pulls the system toward near-black instead of dark olive, which makes it feel more architectural and editorial. The olive becomes a secondary signature colour rather than the dominant one. Beige enters as Warm Sand, used sparingly.

**Typography.**
- Headlines: *ABC Diatype* or *Neue Haas Grotesk Display* (precise modernist sans).
- Body: same family, regular weight.
- Technical accent: a mono face like *JetBrains Mono* or *ABC Diatype Mono* used for labels, dosages, file numbers, dates. This is the signature move of the concept.

**Logo direction.** Subtle redraw. Tighten the silhouette into something more diagrammatic. Two clear vertebra forms instead of puzzle pieces. The wordmark in a precise grotesque, all lowercase, set tighter than current. The descriptor reads "Chiropractic Practice. Bilbao." in mono type.

**Photography.** High-key natural light. Architecture-led. Empty rooms. Hands holding a single tool. Sara photographed from the side, looking at a patient, not the camera. Black and white frequently used.

**Sample copy.** "Restore the signal."

**References.** A.P.C., the Wirth clinic in Berlin, COMO Shambhala's print collateral, the brand work for Nordic medical practices.

**Trade-off.** Distinctive and grown-up. But might feel cold to patients who want softness. Requires the photography and copy to be executed at a high level or it will read as pretentious.

## CONCEPT C — *Living System* (my recommendation, see Part 4)

**The feeling.** An editorial nervous-system practice. The brand is built around the brain-spine connection as its visual language. Anatomical illustration becomes a brand asset, not a textbook reference. Warm and architectural at once.

**The room it evokes.** Beige walls, oak, glass partitions, a deep olive treatment bench, and one large hand-drawn anatomical print of a spine running floor-to-ceiling near reception. Patients see the work depicted before they receive it.

**Palette.**

| Role | Name | Hex |
|------|------|-----|
| Primary background | Bone | #EDE7DA |
| Secondary background | Soft Olive Cream | #F2F4EE |
| Brand colour | Deep Olive Moss | #5F6F4A |
| Anchor / type | Olive Bark | #2F3A23 |
| Warm accent | Clay | #B89576 |
| Quiet contrast | Slate Black | #1F211C |

**Why this palette.** Bone is warmer than off-white and gives the system a beige foundation that ties to the interior materials. Clay is a single warm accent that lets anatomical illustrations sit warmly on the page. Slate Black is reserved for typography only, not large surfaces.

**Typography.**
- Headlines: *GT Sectra* or *Tiempos Headline* (a serif with anatomical contrast in its strokes, fitting for the concept).
- Body: *Söhne* or *Inter*.
- Editorial accent: an italic of the serif, used for pull quotes and the brand-line itself.

**Logo direction.** Keep the silhouette family but redraw with intent. The head form remains. The two puzzle pieces are reimagined as two clear vertebrae flowing from the base of the skull. Outline weight refined to fine line. The mark works in three lockups: stacked, horizontal, and a single anatomical-print version where the spine extends down the page as a graphic device.

**Anatomical illustration as brand asset.** Commission or licence a small set of hand-drawn anatomical drawings (cervical spine, full vertebral column, brain stem, dermatomes). These become the brand's signature visual element. Used at large scale on the website, the wall of the practice, postcards, intake folders. This is the move that lifts the brand above its category.

**Photography.** Natural light, warm. Three subjects: Sara at work, the room, anatomical detail (a hand on a neck, the curve of a spine in good posture). Black and white reserved for the anatomical close-ups.

**Sample copy.** "Care for the line that runs you."

**References.** Hommage Paris, Frama Copenhagen, the book design of Phaidon's anatomical reprints, the brand work of Roman or Hims when they take themselves seriously.

**Trade-off.** Requires investment in the illustration set up front. But that asset then powers Instagram, signage, web, and print for years. Most distinctive of the three directions.

# PART 4 — RECOMMENDATION

I would build Concept C.

**Why.**

The brand idea is *care for the line that runs you*. Concept A illustrates calmness. Concept B illustrates precision. Only Concept C illustrates the line itself. It uses Sara's actual differentiator (nervous system focus) as the visual hero, which is the single hardest thing to do well in this category and the thing most chiropractic brands fail at.

The anatomical illustration asset is the move. Once commissioned, it powers every surface for years and makes the brand instantly identifiable across web, Instagram, signage, intake folders, and the wall of the practice itself. Concept A would need styling props to be photogenic. Concept B would need consistently excellent photography. Concept C carries itself even when the photography is ordinary, because the illustration set does the heavy lifting.

Concept C also solves the logo brief Sara originally asked for. Refining the outlines is not enough to make the current mark communicate brain-spine. Concept C redraws the right-hand shapes as actual vertebrae, which means the silhouette stays familiar to existing patients but the meaning finally lands.

If Sara hates illustration, fall back to Concept B. If Sara wants something safe and immediately likeable, Concept A. If she wants the brand to leave a mark in the category, Concept C is the answer.

The rest of this document details Concept C. The same structure can be adapted if Sara picks A or B.

# PART 5 — VISUAL SYSTEM (Concept C in detail)

## Logo system

**Primary lockup.** The refined silhouette mark sits left of the wordmark. The wordmark "quirosasun" is set in the chosen serif, lowercase, with a fine letterspacing increase from the current execution. The descriptor "Chiropractic Practice. Bilbao." sits below in body sans, tracked wide, in Olive Bark.

**Mark refinements (the spec for a designer).**
- Reduce the outer silhouette's visual weight; remove the solid fill, replace with a fine outlined version on light backgrounds, solid white on dark backgrounds.
- Redraw the two puzzle-piece forms as two stylised vertebrae: rounded vertebral body shapes with subtle transverse processes. They should read as anatomical to anyone paying attention and as graphic to anyone not.
- Maintain the original silhouette's overall proportions so existing patients still recognise the mark.

**Logo variations.**
1. Full lockup (mark + wordmark + descriptor).
2. Wordmark only (for narrow applications and Instagram bio).
3. Mark only (for favicons, social avatars, embroidery).
4. Vertical lockup (mark above wordmark, descriptor centered below).
5. Single-line extended spine version: the mark with its vertebral element extending down vertically as a graphic device, used at large scale on web hero sections and signage.

**Clear space.** Minimum clear space around the lockup equals the height of the lowercase "q" in the wordmark, on all four sides.

**Minimum size.** Mark alone: 24 px digital, 8 mm print. Full lockup: 120 px digital, 30 mm print.

## Colour system in use

| Hex | Role | Usage ratio |
|------|------|------------|
| #EDE7DA Bone | Primary background | ~50% of any surface |
| #F2F4EE Soft Olive Cream | Secondary background, alternate sections | ~20% |
| #5F6F4A Deep Olive Moss | Brand colour, buttons, key elements | ~15% |
| #2F3A23 Olive Bark | Headlines, body type, logo | ~10% |
| #B89576 Clay | Single warm accent, hover states, illustration tone | ~3% |
| #1F211C Slate Black | Reserved type colour for editorial moments | ~2% |

**Rules.**
- Bone or Soft Olive Cream is always the canvas.
- Deep Olive Moss is the only colour used for primary buttons and key brand surfaces.
- Clay is never a button. It is an illustration tone, an accent line, a hover state.
- Slate Black replaces Olive Bark for type only on photography overlays.
- Vivid greens, pure white (#FFFFFF), and pure black (#000000) are not used anywhere in the system.

## Typography in use

**Display.** *GT Sectra Display* or *Tiempos Headline*, both at Regular weight. Headlines set in sentence case, never all caps. Hyphenation off. Tracking at default. Line height generous (1.15-1.25).

**Body.** *Söhne Buch* (or Inter Regular as web fallback). Body copy set at 17-18 px on web, 11-12 pt in print. Line height 1.55. Long-form copy is left-aligned, never justified.

**Editorial accent.** The italic of the display face, used sparingly: pull quotes, the brand line, captions.

**Small labels.** Body face in small caps, tracked +120, used for category labels ("CARE PLAN", "FIRST VISIT", "BILBAO").

**Web fallbacks.** EB Garamond (display fallback), Inter (body fallback). Both are free and excellent.

## Photography direction

**Three categories, in this hierarchy of importance:**

1. **The room.** The new space is a brand asset. Photographed in natural light, mid-morning or late afternoon, never overhead noon. Wide architectural shots. Detail shots of single elements: the corner of an olive table, the edge of a glass partition, a hand on wood. Empty room photography is allowed and encouraged. Black and white reserved for occasional editorial moments.

2. **Sara at work.** Sara photographed in the act of practising, not posing. Looking at a patient, hands at work, mid-conversation, in profile. Never smiling at the camera. Editorial portraiture, closer to a Kinfolk feature than a clinic website. Sara wears the practice uniform (see Part 7) so visual consistency is automatic.

3. **Anatomical detail.** Real bodies, photographed beautifully. The curve of a neck, the line of a shoulder blade, a hand resting on a sacrum. Never gory, never medical-textbook. Always tasteful, always anatomical. Black and white permitted.

**Forbidden.**
- Stock photography of smiling families.
- Sara in a white coat with crossed arms.
- Soft-focus candles, stones, towels rolled into spa shapes.
- Skeletons or graphic medical imagery.
- Phone-camera shots used at brand scale.

## The anatomical illustration set

**The single most important brand investment.** Commission a Spanish or European illustrator with a botanical or anatomical drawing background. Specifications:

- Six core drawings to start: cervical spine, full vertebral column, brain stem and cranial nerves, sacrum and pelvis, side profile of standing posture, side profile of seated posture.
- Hand-drawn in fine line, with subtle warm wash (using Clay #B89576 as a tone).
- Drawings can be used at any scale from postage stamp to wall-sized.
- Style reference: Cécile Hudrisier, Katie Scott, or the older Frank Netter plates done quietly.
- Budget likely 1,500 to 4,000 euros for the set, depending on illustrator. This is the highest-ROI line item in the whole brand build.

These drawings become Instagram tile backgrounds, web section dividers, intake folder covers, treatment room wall art, business card backs, and the central element of the workshop posters.

## Iconography

Minimal. The brand does not need a large icon set. Five icons total:

- Spine (vertical, three vertebra dots).
- Hand (a simple outlined palm).
- Pin (location).
- Clock (time).
- Letter (contact).

All drawn at 1 px line weight, in Olive Bark, square 24x24 grid. Icons never appear in Deep Olive Moss or Clay. The anatomical illustrations do the visual heavy lifting; icons stay strictly utilitarian.

## Patterns and textures

No decorative patterns. The only textural element is a fine horizontal rule (1 px Olive Bark) used to divide editorial sections. Treatment table fabric, wood grain, and the anatomical illustrations provide all the texture the brand needs.

# PART 7 — APPLICATIONS

## Website

The current site is functional but reads as a freelance WordPress template. The new site should be quieter, slower, more architectural. Recommendations:

- One-column layouts. Generous white space. No three-column "features" sections.
- Hero on the homepage: a single anatomical illustration (the full vertebral column) at large scale, with the tagline set in Sectra Italic beside it. Below the fold, a single sentence about the practice and a button.
- Treatment-page structure: not "services" but "how the work goes." First visit, follow-up visits, maintenance care. Written as plain prose, not bullet lists.
- Testimonials presented as long quotes, one per page, with the patient's first name and how long they have been a patient. Not carousels.
- Booking integration: a single, prominent "Book the first conversation" button on every page.
- Mobile-first design.

## Instagram

Current Instagram (assumed from the brief): mixed visual quality, generic chiropractic content. The new account should treat itself like a small editorial magazine.

**Content pillars rotated weekly:**
- The signal (educational posts about the nervous system, posture, breath).
- The long view (mini-essays on spinal care over time).
- The room (photography of the practice).
- Sara at work (portraiture and process).

**Visual rules:**
- Three of every nine tiles should be anatomical illustrations (the commissioned set).
- Three should be photography (room or Sara).
- Three should be type-only posts (a question, a single sentence, a definition).
- Captions written in the brand voice (see Part 6).
- No emojis in body copy. Acceptable only as the single arrow ↓ in CTAs.
- No quote graphics with calligraphy or pastel borders.

## Signage and interior wayfinding

The brand's strongest application. Specifications:

- Practice name in cut metal or vinyl on glass, lowercase wordmark, Olive Bark on Bone background.
- One anatomical illustration framed at floor-to-ceiling scale in the reception area.
- Treatment room signage in tracked small caps on the door.
- Hand-printed cards on the table in each treatment room, listing the technique used that day.

## Print collateral

Minimum set for launch:

1. **Business cards.** Bone front with full lockup. Olive Bark back with the tagline in italic, plus one small anatomical illustration in Clay.
2. **First-visit folder.** A simple A4 folder in Bone, embossed lowercase logo on the front, containing intake form, care-plan template, and a one-page explanation of the work.
3. **Care-plan document.** A two-page printed plan handed to each patient after the second visit, listing recommended visit frequency and key observations. Designed, not photocopied.
4. **Workshop poster.** A3 format, single anatomical illustration as background, tagline and event details set quietly over the top.
5. **Postcards.** Six postcard designs, each featuring one of the commissioned anatomical illustrations on the front and a single brand line on the back. Used as reminder cards and as gifts.

## Practice uniform

A small but meaningful brand surface. Sara and any future staff wear:
- Linen apron in Bone or Soft Olive Cream over their own clothing, with the small mark embroidered at the chest in Olive Bark.
- Or: a relaxed-fit linen shirt in the same colours, no logo, with a small brand pin.

Never a white medical coat.

# PART 8 — DO'S AND DON'TS

## Do

- Use Bone or Soft Olive Cream as the canvas for every brand surface.
- Pair Sectra Italic with Söhne Regular. They are the brand's voice in type form.
- Use the anatomical illustration set generously. It is the brand's signature.
- Write copy that respects the reader's intelligence.
- Leave white space.
- Photograph in natural light only.
- Use lowercase for the wordmark always.

## Don't

- Do not use the vivid emerald green from the current logo. It is retired.
- Do not use pure white (#FFFFFF) or pure black (#000000) for backgrounds or large surfaces.
- Do not use Clay as a button colour or for type. It is illustration only.
- Do not stretch, recolour, or place the logo on busy photography without a tint layer.
- Do not use stock photography.
- Do not use wellness clichés in copy.
- Do not use exclamation marks in brand voice.
- Do not place the logo smaller than the minimums specified.
- Do not let the logo touch the edge of any layout (respect clear space).
- Do not add a tagline or descriptor next to the logo unless it is the approved version.
- Do not use the Sectra serif for body copy. It is a display face only.
- Do not justify long-form copy.

**Brand:** Quirosasun, a chiropractic practice in Bilbao, Spain.

**Tone:** Quiet, architectural, editorial. Premium but not luxury. Clinical but warm. Closer to a Japanese clinic or a Scandinavian dental practice than a wellness spa.

**Mood:** Calm, slow morning light, natural materials, considered. The viewer should feel they are entering a place where adults take their bodies seriously.

**Colour palette (use these exact hex values where possible):**
- Bone (#EDE7DA): primary background, warm beige.
- Soft Olive Cream (#F2F4EE): secondary background, pale green-cream.
- Deep Olive Moss (#5F6F4A): brand colour, used for furniture and accents.
- Olive Bark (#2F3A23): near-black green, for type.
- Clay (#B89576): single warm accent, for illustration tone.
- Slate Black (#1F211C): reserved for type on photography.
- Forbidden: pure white, pure black, vivid emerald green, pastel pink, baby blue.

**Materials in the practice:** oak wood, glass partitions, black steel, linen, ceramic, the deep olive treatment table. No plastic, no chrome, no marble.

**Photography style:**
- Always natural light, mid-morning or late afternoon.
- Never overhead noon light.
- Wide architectural shots and tight detail shots, no medium "lifestyle" framing.
- Subjects: the practice interior, hands at work on a spine, anatomical details (necks, shoulders, sacrums), Sara at work in profile.
- Sara wears a linen apron in Bone over neutral clothing. Never a white medical coat.
- Black and white acceptable for anatomical close-ups only.
- Forbidden: smiling-to-camera, stock-style families, candles, towels rolled spa-style, soft focus, lens flare, vivid colours, plastic medical equipment in frame.

**Typography:**
- Headlines in a serif with anatomical contrast (GT Sectra, Tiempos, or similar).
- Body in a clean grotesque (Söhne, Inter, or similar).
- Logo wordmark is always lowercase: *quirosasun*.

**Logo:** A refined silhouette of a head profile connected to two stylised vertebra forms at the lower right, in fine outline. The wordmark "quirosasun" sits beside or below in the serif face. Always shown in Olive Bark on light backgrounds or Bone on dark backgrounds. Never on vivid green.

**Signature brand element:** Hand-drawn anatomical illustrations of the spine, brain stem, vertebrae, and posture, in fine line with a warm Clay-toned wash. These appear large on walls, on social media, on print collateral.

**Avoid generating:** anything that looks like a typical wellness brand (candles, stones, sunset yoga), anything that looks like a typical medical brand (white coats, stethoscopes, plus signs), anything cartoonish, anything with vivid colours, anything with stock-photo smiling people.

**Voice for caption / copy:** Specific, calm, dry, anatomical. No exclamation marks. No wellness clichés. Examples: "Care for the line that runs you." "Restore the signal." "The spine carries about a hundred million signals an hour."
