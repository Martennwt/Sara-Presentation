Current brand audit · Quirosasun

A strong foundation
asking for more.

What quirosasun.com communicates today — section by section. Where it builds trust, where it loses it, and where the biggest opportunities are waiting.

01 · Hero

What it communicates
  • Local and findable. "Quiropractico Bilbao" positions clearly for search — visitors know immediately where this practice is.
  • Real people, real work. The photography shows Sara in an actual session — not stock, not staged. That's rare and valuable.
  • Approachable and accessible. The overall tone is friendly, not cold or clinical.
Weak points
  • The green overlay buries the photography. The strongest asset on the site — Sara in a real session — is filtered green. It reads as a health app, not a specialist practice.
  • The logo signals the wrong category. A green puzzle-piece icon reads as health insurance or a wellness app. Not a serious chiropractic specialist.
  • "Quiropractico Bilbao" is an SEO headline, not a brand statement. It could belong to any of the twenty other practices in the city. No emotional hook. No differentiation.
  • Green on green on green. Logo, overlay, and CTA button all share the same vivid tone — the eye has nowhere to land.
Potential
  • Remove the tint and let the photography breathe. The session images are genuinely compelling — they don't need filtering.
  • A single line that no other chiropractor in Bilbao can say would change everything. The headline is the biggest single lever on the entire site.
  • A refined logo and restrained palette would lift the first impression from "local clinic" to "specialist you trust before you've read a word."

02 + 03 · About Sara & Conditions

What it communicates
  • Sara is the practice. "Soy Sara" is exactly the right instinct — personal, warm, direct. The visitor knows immediately who they're dealing with.
  • Credentials are present. Barcelona College, master's from Pompeu Fabra, doctorate from the European Academy — the qualifications are visible.
  • Broad clinical scope. The scrolling conditions marquee signals that the practice handles many situations, not just back pain.
Weak points
  • "De confianza" is the most overused phrase in Spanish service businesses. Every dentist, mechanic, and lawyer uses it. It says nothing specific about Sara.
  • The credentials are listed, but the story isn't told. Why chiropractic? Why Bilbao? Her most powerful brand asset — a personal origin story — is buried on a separate page.
  • The conditions marquee is pure SEO logic, not patient logic. No patient thinks "I have dolor de cuello, let me find a site that lists that word." It reads as a keyword dump, not a human practice.
  • Massive bold caps scrolling across the screen clashes with the quiet warmth above it. The tonal break is jarring.
Potential
  • Her father's story — discovering chiropractic at 15, the decision that shaped her — is rare brand material. It should anchor this section, not sit hidden in "Sobre Mí."
  • Replace the conditions list with outcome language: "You came in barely able to turn your head. You left standing straighter." Same information, written for the patient.

04 · Educational content

What it communicates
  • Sara explains, not just adjusts. The spine model and the clinical setting signal an educational, thoughtful approach to care.
  • Real environment, real tools. The props, the patient, the setting — all authentic. Nothing staged.
  • She invests in patient education. A practitioner who takes time to explain earns a different kind of trust.
Weak points
  • The section drops in without context. No headline, no framing — the visitor doesn't know what they're about to see or why it matters to them.
  • No copy bridges the content to the patient's situation. A powerful image is left to carry everything alone.
  • The branded card visible on the monitor clashes with the site's visual system — a small but telling sign that the brand isn't yet unified across touchpoints.
Potential
  • A single framing headline unlocks this section: "Understanding your spine changes how you live in your body."
  • A pull-quote from Sara — something she actually says — could prime the visitor before they watch and make the content land harder.
  • This section, handled well, is a trust-builder unlike anything else on the page. Most practitioners don't explain. Sara does.

05 · Testimonials

What it communicates
  • Patients have transformative, emotional experiences. The reviews are long, specific, and personal — not generic stars.
  • The practice generates real loyalty. "Con ganas de seguir mi tratamiento" — patients don't want to stop. That's a rare signal.
  • Outcomes are life-scale, not symptom-scale. "Cambiar mi relación con mi cuerpo" is a brand-level claim that patients are making on Sara's behalf — unprompted.
Weak points
  • "Esto es lo que Opinan mis Clientes…" is placeholder copy. It describes what you're about to read without inviting you in. It doesn't honor the quality of the reviews beneath it.
  • The slider format hides the strongest asset on the site. Only two reviews visible at a time. The content is exceptional — and it's being treated as an afterthought.
  • Visual hierarchy is flat. Every word in every review carries the same weight. Nothing pulls out. Nothing lands harder than anything else.
Potential
  • Lead with a full-width pull-quote from the best review, typeset large — before any heading. Let the patient speak first.
  • Display at least three reviews in a grid. The content is strong enough to fill the space and the more a visitor reads, the more they trust.
  • Rename the section. "In their words." or "What changes." does more work in two words than the current heading does in seven.

06 · FAQ

What it communicates
  • Sara anticipates objections and is transparent. The FAQ shows real awareness of what first-time patients fear — pain, uncertainty, frequency — and answers honestly.
  • Educational and non-intimidating. A practice that explains this clearly is a practice that respects its patients.
  • The questions are the right questions. "Are adjustments painful?" and "Can I go if I'm pregnant?" are exactly what an anxious first-time visitor needs answered.
Weak points
  • Visually, this is the most off-brand section on the site. Green rounded accordion panels look like a generic Elementor healthcare template — no connection to the warmth or restraint of the sections above.
  • Typography hierarchy collapses. Questions and answers share near-identical visual weight. The eye doesn't know where to land.
  • Green saturation peaks exactly here. The accordion headers are full vivid green — it overwhelms the content and clashes entirely with the soft, human tone the practice wants to project.
  • "Preguntas Frecuentes" is a label, not an invitation. It does nothing for the curious, anxious, or undecided visitor.
Potential
  • The content is strong — it only needs a visual system to carry it. A typography-driven design without accordion widgets would feel significantly more premium.
  • Rename to signal care: "Before your first visit" or "What patients usually ask" — same content, completely different emotional entry point.
  • This is the last piece of persuasion before contact. It should close with a direct CTA: "Ready? Book your first visit."

07 · Location & closing

What it communicates
  • Local and findable. The map places the practice clearly in Bilbao — useful for patients gauging distance.
  • Functional and direct. No ambiguity about where to go.
Weak points
  • The site ends with a Google Maps widget. After building warmth through Sara's story, testimonials, and FAQ, the page closes with a utility embed. It's an anticlimax.
  • No address in text — only on the map. A visitor on mobile who wants to copy or screenshot an address has nowhere to look.
  • No closing voice. Sara doesn't say goodbye. There's no sign-off, no final line that stays with the visitor after they close the tab.
  • No final CTA. The page's last action is "click the map" — not "book an appointment."
Potential
  • Close with Sara's voice before the map: "The first step is often the hardest. I'll take care of the rest."
  • Show address, phone, and hours in readable text above the embed — easy to copy, screenshot, and share.
  • A prominent "Book a first visit" button here, after all the trust has been built, would convert everything the page worked toward. Right now that moment is left on the table.
